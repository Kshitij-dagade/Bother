# BOTHER — Google Stitch Design Brief

## 1. What to design

Design a polished, responsive **waitlist landing page** for BOTHER, an upcoming problem-first social platform. The immediate goal is to validate demand and convert interested visitors into waitlist sign-ups—not to design the full social app in this task.

Create a high-fidelity, modern website with desktop and mobile layouts. It should feel like a credible, thoughtfully designed consumer-tech brand: warm, human, emotionally relatable, clear, and distinctive. Prioritize conversion and message clarity over decorative complexity.

**Working brand name:** BOTHER  
**Primary tagline:** Everyone has something.  
**Primary campaign line:** You're not the only one.  
**Core promise:** Share what's bothering you, find people who've been through it, and discover what actually helped.  
**Primary CTA:** Join the waitlist  
**Secondary CTA:** See how it works

BOTHER is a working name; do not add trademark claims or imply that the brand name is legally cleared.

---

## 2. Product context

BOTHER is a free, problem-first social network. Instead of centering the experience on followers, viral posts, or generic Q&A, it centers on real-life problems and the people who have experienced them.

A visitor should understand the idea in a few seconds:

1. Everyone has things they're trying to figure out.
2. Other people have often faced similar situations.
3. BOTHER helps people find those people, hear real experiences, and learn what others tried.
4. The platform is intended to be free for end users.

Example problem areas include career uncertainty, friendships, relationships, moving to a new city, money decisions, everyday life choices, shopping decisions, and fitness goals. Do not make the site feel clinical, like a therapy service, or like a generic advice marketplace.

**Positioning:** A social network for the things you don't know how to figure out alone.  
**Emotional benefit:** “I don't have to figure this out alone.”  
**Functional benefit:** Find people dealing with a similar situation and learn from experiences, approaches, and outcomes.

---

## 3. Brand personality

The brand should feel:

- Human and empathetic, without being sentimental.
- Honest and non-judgmental.
- Curious, relatable, and quietly optimistic.
- Contemporary and digitally native.
- Credible and safe, without looking corporate or clinical.

Avoid:

- Therapy-clinic visuals or medical iconography.
- Overly cheerful “good vibes only” messaging.
- Startup clichés such as generic gradients, floating blobs, glassmorphism everywhere, or exaggerated 3D objects.
- Aggressive growth-hacking patterns, fake counters, fake testimonials, or urgency timers.
- A visual style that looks like a clone of Reddit, Quora, X, Instagram, or LinkedIn.

---

## 4. Visual identity

Use this as the initial design system and apply it consistently across the page.

### Colors

- **Deep Indigo — primary:** `#28244A`  
  Use for key headings, primary buttons, logo, and strong text accents.
- **Electric Coral — accent:** `#FF6B5A`  
  Use sparingly for small emphasis, highlighted words, active states, and important visual details.
- **Fresh Mint — positive/action status:** `#43C6A5`  
  Use only for progress/positive cues, not as the main brand color.
- **Warm Ivory — page background:** `#F8F7F2`  
  Main background to feel warmer and more approachable than pure white.
- **Ink — body text:** `#1E1E24`  
  Use for readable body copy.
- **Muted Gray — secondary text:** approximately `#777681`.
- **White — cards and form surfaces:** `#FFFFFF`.
- **Border — subtle divider:** approximately `#E7E4E0`.

Maintain strong color contrast and accessible text/button contrast. Use coral as an accent, not as a giant background across the entire page. The layout should mostly use warm ivory, white surfaces, and indigo typography.

### Typography

Use a modern, highly legible sans-serif family. Prefer **Inter**, **Manrope**, or a visually similar available font. Use confident, editorial headline typography and calm, spacious body typography.

- Hero headline: large, bold, concise; desktop around 60–76 px, mobile around 40–48 px.
- Section headings: around 32–44 px desktop, scaled down responsively.
- Body text: around 16–19 px, comfortable line height.
- Supporting labels: around 12–14 px, never too faint.

Use sentence case rather than excessive all-caps. Keep line lengths short and readable.

### Logo direction

Use a clean lowercase wordmark: **bother.** The wordmark should feel friendly, modern, and memorable. A small imperfect circle can be explored as a brand symbol for an unresolved problem, but do not let the logo become a complicated illustration. If a finished logo cannot be created reliably, use a clean text-based wordmark rather than inventing a low-quality symbol.

### Shape and layout language

- Soft, intentional corner radii: 12–20 px for cards and input fields.
- Pill shape for the main CTA is acceptable, but avoid making every element a pill.
- Spacious layouts with clear hierarchy.
- Thin borders and subtle shadows; avoid heavy drop shadows.
- Use a consistent spacing scale based on 8 px.
- Use simple line icons only when they improve understanding.
